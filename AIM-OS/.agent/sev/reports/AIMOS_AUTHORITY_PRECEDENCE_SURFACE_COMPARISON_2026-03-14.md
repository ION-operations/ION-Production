## Direct Comparative Reading

- Root host-control surfaces are strongest when the question is "which lane and workspace law even applies here?"
- Startup and doctrine surfaces are strongest when the question is "how should this lane be executed and coordinated once routed?"
- Correction packets are strongest when the question is "which earlier framing was explicitly corrected and how should older work now be read?"
- Active work-package law surfaces are strongest when the question is "what exactly is authorized for this turn right now?"
- Artifact-local boundaries are strongest when the question is "what does this specific artifact mean, and how far can its language be stretched?"

## Visible Authority And Precedence Contradictions

1. `AGENTS.md`, `.agent/STARTUP.md`, and `.agent/COMMS_DOCTRINE.md` all use mandatory control language, so the authority stack is visibly plural rather than singular at the file level.
2. `AGENTS.md` duplicates the consolidation-decision-freeze block, which is local control-surface drift inside the root host-control family even though the substantive constraint is the same in both copies.
3. The correction packet uses immediate override language against stale framing and operator-question dumping, but its correction scope is targeted rather than organism-wide, so it is strong corrective law without being self-evidently total authority.
4. Active work-package files all present mandatory law in nearly identical shapes, which means filesystem presence alone does not reveal current precedence; the live bus directive is what made WP26 active over WP25 and WP24 on 2026-03-14.
5. Artifact-local boundaries are the sharpest semantic constraints inside a report, but they cannot by themselves answer whether root routing, doctrine, correction packets, or active packet law should outrank one another across the lane.

## Evidence Boundaries

- Root host-control surfaces were treated as host-routing and workspace-control law, not as proof of every packet-level permission.
- Startup or doctrine surfaces were treated as operational law, not as automatic override of all packet-specific scope.
- Correction-packet surfaces were treated as targeted override law for named failures, not as universal precedence canon.
- Active work-package law surfaces were treated as current-turn authorization, not as total replacement of root or doctrinal control.
- Artifact-local boundary surfaces were treated as local semantic constraint only, not as lane-wide authority ordering.

## Direct Comparative Reading

### Identity router vs comms route tables

- The identity router is strongest at answering "who am I in this host?"
- The comms route-table family is strongest at answering "what canonical sender ID and filesystem route key should represent this lane?"
- They complement each other, but they do not cover exactly the same set of lanes; `COMPOSER-SEV` is the clearest gap.

### Root hierarchy vs genome identity/network

- Root hierarchy surfaces are strongest at broad authority and command-chain framing.
- Genome identity/network surfaces are strongest at lane-local self-understanding, peer relations, and scope law.
- The OPUS lane is the sharpest mismatch: the root north-star layer frames COO hierarchy, while the genome layer still frames autonomous acting-command authority.

### Route tables vs route-bearing filesystem surfaces

- Route tables show intended canonical routing.
- Filesystem surfaces show realized routing.
- The OPUS lane proves why the distinction matters: canonical comms routing says `antigravity`, while actual stored continuity spans `antigravity` status/inbox and `opus` chat/capsules/inbox.

## Net Comparative Answer

1. Workspace identity-router surfaces anchor host-to-callsign binding.
2. Comms canonical identity and route-table surfaces anchor canonical IDs, aliases, and route-key law.
3. Root hierarchy and command-chain surfaces anchor rank and authority structure.
4. Per-agent genome identity or network surfaces anchor lane-local self and peer structure.
5. Route-bearing filesystem surfaces anchor what the force actually looks like on disk.

These are comparative role answers only. They do not select a final route canon or roster winner.
